# Oath Citizenship — color palette rationale

Hans asked why these colors. Every choice below is tied to a real artifact of
US citizenship, not a gradient generator.

**Old Glory Blue `#0A3161`** — the US flag's blue, PMS 282C per the State
Department spec (the flag's colors stand for vigilance, perseverance, and
justice). It is the primary brand color: buttons, headings, navigation,
the mock-interview card. Blue carries the authority of the nation itself,
which is exactly what a citizenship app should feel like.

**Old Glory Red `#B31942`** — the flag's red, PMS 193C (hardiness and valor).
Used sparingly on purpose: the star in the seal, the active tab icon, and
wrong-answer states. It is also the red of the certificate numbers printed
in red ink on every Certificate of Naturalization since 1906, so red means
"official mark" here, not "danger."

**Parchment `#F5EEDA`** — the page background. Aged document stock, the tone
of the Declaration and the certificate paper. Warm and low-glare, which
matters because people study here for hours; pure white fatigues the eyes.

**Iron-gall ink `#23211B`** — body text. The warm near-black of the ink used
on historical documents. Softer than pure black against parchment, and it
keeps the whole app feeling like one coherent document.

**Great Seal gold `#A8801F`** — reserved for *earned* moments only: progress
rings, the mock-interview button, achievement states. On the real certificate
the Great Seal is stamped in gold foil, so gold here reads as a foil stamp —
something you earn, not decoration. It is deliberately never used for plain
UI chrome.

**Certificate green `#2E6B3E`** — correct-answer states, taken from the green
guilloche (anti-counterfeit line patterns) on the naturalization certificate.
Green already means "correct" everywhere; anchoring it to the certificate
keeps the palette to one story.

**What was removed and why:** the old design's orange gradients and coral
buttons had no civic referent — they were decoration. The navy-to-navy header
gradient was replaced with two crisp stripes (blue over red, like bunting),
because a hard stripe is a deliberate motif while a gradient is a default.
