# Palette rationale — creative-experiments, "black box" gallery (2026-10-10)

Hans flagged a pattern: too many redesigns leaned on warm paper/parchment + ink. For an art gallery, that pattern actively hurts — cream walls compete with the artwork for attention. The new direction is a **black-box gallery**: near-black walls (`#0d0d10`) with warm-white Fraunces type, the way digital art is actually exhibited (teamLab-style dark rooms).

Why it fits this project specifically:
1. **The art becomes the light source.** All three pieces are luminous, saturated work — glowing jellies in deep water, a bright green salamander, vivid kente cloth. Against near-black they glow; against cream paper they flatten.
2. **It matches the pieces' native darkness.** The jellyfish study already runs in deep blue water; dark gallery chrome recedes into that world instead of framing it in an unrelated cream border.
3. **Pattern break.** This is now the only redesign in the set on a dark ground — instantly distinguishable from the paper-toned JotRelay, oath-citizenship, and Bridge.
4. **The specimen-placard concept survives.** The "field notes" idea (numbered plates, mono captions, per-piece accents) is kept, but as dark gallery wall labels: hairline `#26262b` rules, soft warm-gray mono text. Per-piece accents were brightened for dark ground — jelly blue `#5b8fc4`, salamander green `#35b184`, kente gold `#d9a441` — so each plate still carries its own color identity.
5. **Restraint elsewhere.** Typography (Fraunces + Plex Mono, self-hosted) and layout are unchanged — the color was the complaint, not the type or structure. Gold is reserved for the pin and earned-feeling details, never as a background wash. No gradients, no indigo, no AI-slop signifiers.

Artwork files untouched (byte-identical); some pieces are incomplete and will be revisited with Hans later.
